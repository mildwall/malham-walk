Is Appleby worth it, or more stops?

Appleby is mostly worth it as the turning point. Going that far is what gets you the best northern part of the line in daylight: Garsdale, then Ais Gill summit (the highest point of the line), then down the Mallerstang and Eden valleys. The town itself is pleasant for 1–2 hours but isn't a destination on its own.

More stops cost a lot of time. On Sundays trains run only every 2–3 hours each way, so every extra stop costs that long. With sunset at 16:35, the most you can fit is about three stops, and then each one is short or has a fixed length.

My pick is Plan A. It has the most even balance of sights. Plan B is better if the morning is clear and you want a longer walk without racing sunset. C has the most stops but skips the summit section. D is for rain. All four get you back to Shipley at 17:48, and they're compared on the Plans tab.

Walks from the station

A · Viaduct close-up and back ≈3 km · 1 h

Go down from the station to the road junction by the Station Inn. Take the wide gravel track north-west across the moor to the foot of the viaduct (24 arches, about 400 m long), then come back the same way. It's flat and easy; the track can be muddy.

B · On to Blea Moor signal box ≈6–7 km · 1 h 45 – 2 h

As A, then follow the path beside the railway on its east side, north to Blea Moor signal box below the tunnel mouth. Come back the same way. It's a gentle rise, rough in places and boggy after rain. Whernside fills the view.

Turn round at the signal box at least 1 hour before your train. The Leave by tile above works it out from GPS.

Not today: Whernside

The circuits over Whernside or round Blea Moor are 13–17 km and take 4½–6 h. They don't fit any Sunday gap in daylight.

Ribblehead practicalities

  • Lunch at the Station Inn (a few minutes from the platform, at the B6255/B6479 junction). It's listed as open Sundays 10:00–22:00 with food 12:00–20:30. The Sunday roast comes from the specials board, and it has advertised a £10 roast this year; not confirmed for this Sunday. A pub main plus a drink is about £20. Phone 015242 41274. It's also the warm place to wait if you miss a train.
  • The platform has only a small shelter, and mobile signal can be patchy. This page works offline once it's been opened online.
  • It's about 300 m up: colder and windier than Shipley. Bring a torch for any train after 16:30.

Settle: things to tick off

The station is on the edge of the town centre, about 5 min from the Market Place. Used in Plans C and D.

Eating in Settle

  • About £10: Ye Olde Naked Man café and bakery, Market Place. Listed as open daily 8:00–17:00, with a light lunch menu. No prices online. 01729 823230.
  • About £10–20: The Golden Lion, Duke Street. It advertises a menu at 1 course £12, 2 for £15, 3 for £18, served from midday daily. Its autumn menu has soup at £7.50 and mains at £16–18.50 (its £10.50 sandwiches are Monday–Saturday only).

1 November is the first Sunday, so the monthly artisan market (second Sundays) isn't on. Check Sunday hours for cafés and the Folly before relying on them.

Tickets

For every plan here, buy an Off-Peak Day Return Shipley ↔ Appleby (via Skipton). It's quoted at £29.50 for this date, ⅓ off with a railcard. All the stops are on that route, so getting off and back on should count as a normal break of journey. Confirm with the conductor or ticket office before your first stop.

Check before you go

  • Sunday cancellations: since December 2024 Northern has run fewer Sunday trains in the North West because it's short of conductors. Check the live boards on the morning, and before each stop.
  • Weather: one model forecast (TheWeatherOutlook) says about 9 °C, light wind (gusts ~17 mph), dry, 94 % humidity under high pressure. That usually means dry but possibly misty or low cloud, often worst in the morning, which is a point in favour of Plan A's afternoon walk. Take a hat, gloves, waterproof and a torch.
  • Seats: going north, sitting on the left is the usual tip for views down Dentdale.
