Plan A or Plan B?

Appleby is mostly worth it as the turning point. Going that far is what gets you the best northern part of the line in daylight: Garsdale, then Ais Gill summit (the highest point of the line), then down the Mallerstang and Eden valleys. The town itself is pleasant for 1–2 hours but isn't a destination on its own.

My pick is now Plan B. Plan A has two weak points that B avoids. First, everything in A depends on the 13:07 from Appleby. If that Sunday train is cancelled, the next one is 16:05, which reaches Ribblehead at 16:44, after sunset, and the walk is lost. Second, A's walk finishes at sunset with one train home; miss the 16:44 and you wait until 18:51 in the dark on an exposed moor. In B, a cancellation costs you Appleby, not the walk. Missing the 16:05 from Appleby is easy: the 16:45 and 18:10 follow, from a town with cafés.

What A does better: you see the Appleby–Ribblehead stretch a second time on the way home in daylight. In B that ride home is in dusk, but you've already seen all of it going north. And if there's morning mist, A's afternoon walk is more likely to be clear.

You don't have to decide now. Both plans start on the same 09:22 with the same ticket. Look out of the window around Horton (10:25). If you can see the hills and the viaduct, get off at Ribblehead at 10:33 (Plan B). If it's thick mist, stay on to Appleby (Plan A). Then tap the plan here.

Two ways to do the day

Both start on the 09:22 from Shipley (the 11:28 is the only other morning train). Both end on the same train home (Appleby 16:05, Ribblehead 16:44), arriving at Shipley at 17:48. Same ticket for both. Tap "Follow this plan" to drive the Overview tab.

Appleby: things to tick off

In walking order: a loop of about 2 km and 30 min of actual walking (see the map above). From the station you go down to the river and church, up Boroughgate to the castle gate, then back. Tap an item to tick it; ticks are saved on this phone.

The walk back up to the station is 430 m uphill, about 10 min. Opening times come from the places' own websites and guides, not confirmed for this Sunday.

Walks from the station

A · Viaduct close-up and back 1.9 km · about 30 min

Go down from the station to the road junction by the Station Inn. Take the wide gravel track north-west across the moor to the foot of the viaduct (24 arches, about 400 m long), then come back the same way. It's flat and easy; the track can be muddy.

B · On to Blea Moor signal box 4.1 km · about 1 h 05

As A, then follow the track beside the railway north to Blea Moor signal box, the orange line on the map. Come back the same way. It's a gentle rise, rough in places and boggy after rain. Whernside fills the view.

The signal box is about 2 km from the platform, so turn round there at least 40 min before your train. The Leave by tile above works it out from GPS.

Not today: Whernside

The circuits over Whernside or round Blea Moor are 13–17 km and take 4½–6 h. They don't fit any Sunday gap in daylight.

Ribblehead practicalities

  • Lunch at the Station Inn (a few minutes from the platform, at the B6255/B6479 junction). It's listed as open Sundays 10:00–22:00 with food 12:00–20:30. The Sunday roast comes from the specials board, and it has advertised a £10 roast this year; not confirmed for this Sunday. A pub main plus a drink is about £20. Phone 015242 41274. It's also the warm place to wait if you miss a train.
  • The platform has only a small shelter, and mobile signal can be patchy. This page works offline once it's been opened online.
  • It's about 300 m up: colder and windier than Shipley. Bring a torch for any train after 16:30.

Tickets

For every plan here, buy an Off-Peak Day Return Shipley ↔ Appleby (via Skipton). It's quoted at £29.50 for this date, ⅓ off with a railcard. All the stops are on that route, so getting off and back on should count as a normal break of journey. Confirm with the conductor or ticket office before your first stop.

Check before you go

  • Sunday cancellations: since December 2024 Northern has run fewer Sunday trains in the North West because it's short of conductors. Check the live boards on the morning, and before each stop.
  • Weather: one model forecast (TheWeatherOutlook) says about 9 °C, light wind (gusts ~17 mph), dry, 94 % humidity under high pressure. That usually means dry but possibly misty or low cloud, often worst in the morning, so look out of the window at Horton before choosing between Plan A and B (see Overview). Take a hat, gloves, waterproof and a torch.
  • Seats: going north, sitting on the left is the usual tip for views down Dentdale.
